About

Developing computational methods for real-world engineering problems.

My interest in computational engineering began with finite element analysis, computational fluid dynamics, and design optimization. Projects in structural optimization and excavator payload estimation taught me that choosing a method is only part of the challenge: the physical model, available data, and numerical approach have to work together.

In my engineering analysis, I start with physical reasoning and use data to refine what a model can explain. I’m especially interested in models that remain interpretable and useful beyond a single test case. That means questioning assumptions, revisiting model structure, and validating results.

I chose graduate study for research mentorship and deeper training in computational design, nonlinear dynamics, and physics-informed optimization. I joined the Design Systems Lab at Texas A&M to pursue research aligned with my goal of developing computational methods for multidisciplinary engineering problems. My long-term goal is to pursue a career in research and development in industry. I hold myself to high standards and see difficult problems through to results I can explain and defend.

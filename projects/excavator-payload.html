Project overview

The project focused on estimating excavator payload during material-loading operations using onboard hydraulic-pressure and machine-motion measurements. It combined surrogate modeling, signal processing, software development, and equipment integration.

My contribution

My contribution focused on computational modeling, calibration, signal processing, and simulation. During senior design, I developed the Python calibration and payload-estimation workflow for a prescribed lift. In the subsequent R&D phase, I developed an inverse surrogate model incorporating regularized regression and motion compensation, and evaluated its behavior through simulations based on recorded field data.

This computational work supported the team’s broader excavator weighing system, which included sensor hardware, a touchscreen interface, installation kits, and field testing.

From capstone to industry R&D

Senior design | September 2025–May 2026

The capstone team’s approximately $2,500 prototype combined hydraulic pressure transducers, dynamic inclinometers, a Raspberry Pi, and an in-cab touchscreen.

I developed a Python calibration and payload-estimation workflow using hydraulic pressure, boom and stick positions, and engine speed during a prescribed lift, without direct geometric measurements. The team field-tested the prototype across five payload levels and six engine-speed settings.

Industry R&D | June–August 2026

Working with Michael Wilson and Connor Luce, I focused on the inverse surrogate model in Python, calibration-based regularized regression, and motion compensation. I implemented signal filtering, outlier rejection, and measurement-stability checks to support consistent payload estimates.

Team context: The broader project included a Raspberry Pi touchscreen for bucket weighing and truck-load tracking, sensor integration, installation kits, and field testing on CAT 335F and CAT 374F excavators.

These computational methods estimate payload from hydraulic pressure, boom and stick orientation, and motion measurements.

Simulations based on recorded field data

Model behavior was evaluated through 820 replays across 164 unique captures and five calibration scenarios. A further 2,750 sensor-disturbance cases generated from 11 representative recordings examined how sensor bias and noise affect payload estimates.

These counts describe the scope of the simulations, not additional physical lifts or a final field-accuracy result.
